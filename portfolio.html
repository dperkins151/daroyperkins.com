06 Oxon Run Community Solar — 2.65 MW ground mount

Superintendent — full build, GRID Alternatives · Washington, DC (Ward 8) · 2020

Ran the full build on a 15-acre brownfield in Oxon Run Park: roughly 7,280 modules at fixed tilt, 2.65 MW DC / 2.0 MW AC, for the DC Department of General Services. At completion it was the largest community solar system in the District — free electricity for about 750 income-qualifying households through Solar for All, offsetting roughly $500 a year on each household's bill.

07 Thurman Brisben Center — 115.2 kW rooftop, on a homeless shelter

Electrical install, GRID Alternatives · Fredericksburg, VA · 2020

Solar on the roof of the Thurman Brisben Center, Fredericksburg's homeless shelter — a 115.2 kW system, at its unveiling the largest rooftop array in the city. It covers about 75% of the center's energy needs and cut roughly $1,400 a month off the shelter's electric bill. My photos are the ground-level electrical work: the parallel conduit home runs I ran down the cinder-block wall, bent around the corner and dropped to the inverter bank. Still the install I point to when someone asks which one I'm proudest of — the power bill it erased belongs to a shelter.

08 Our Lady of Perpetual Help Church — rooftop solar, approx. 150–200 kW

Superintendent — Neo Energy · Washington, DC (Southeast / Anacostia) · 2022

Ballasted rooftop arrays across the Our Lady of Perpetual Help campus in Anacostia — roughly 150–200 kW spread over the membrane roofs of the church buildings, including the Panorama Room hall. The parish is known as "The Church on The Hill"; from the hall the DC skyline and the Washington Monument sit in plain view.

09 Brentwood Reservoir Community Solar — 1.8 MW atop a drinking-water reservoir

Superintendent — Neo Energy · Washington, DC (Northeast / Brentwood) · 2022

A 1.8 MW community solar build on an 18-acre DC Water site with nothing under it but drinking water: the array sits on the cap of the Brentwood Reservoir, a buried concrete reservoir holding about 25 million gallons, in service since 1959, with more panels ground-mounted down the south-facing slope. Roughly 4,000 panels, built for NHT Ingenuity Power and SunLight General Capital under a DC Water site license. The output — about 2.5 million kWh a year — goes 100% free to more than 500 low-to-moderate-income DC households through Solar for All.

10 Worthington Woods — 2.4 MW across a whole neighborhood's rooftops

Superintendent — Neo Energy · Washington, DC (Ward 8) · 2023

Not one roof — dozens. 2.4 MW of rooftop solar spread across the low-rise buildings of Worthington Woods Apartments, a 394-unit affordable-housing garden community at 4419 3rd St SE: 14 acres, built in 1944, owned by Montgomery Housing Partnership. NEO and Access TAB won the District's RFP for the install, and the panels were in place ahead of MHP's October 2023 renovation groundbreaking. Every home in the community is reserved for residents earning at or below 60% of area median income — and the solar is there to push their utility costs down.

11 Ridgecrest Village — 1.855 MW covering 100% of a community's power

Superintendent — Neo Energy · Washington, DC (Ward 8 / Congress Heights) · 2023

Rooftops and carports, one system. At Ridgecrest Village Apartments — a 272-unit affordable community in Congress Heights, built in 1951 and owned by The NHP Foundation — NEO put a 1,855 kW PV system across the property's roofs and parking canopies. It was sized to produce enough energy to cover 100% of the property's energy needs, cutting energy costs roughly in half — savings that stay inside an affordable-housing budget.
