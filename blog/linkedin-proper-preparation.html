Proper preparation prevents poor performance.

Five words that decide how a job goes before the first panel ever lands on the roof.

When a day falls apart in the field, it almost never starts that day. It started the night before, when nobody walked the drawings. Or at 6 AM, when the material count was assumed instead of checked. By 10 AM the crew is standing around waiting on what should have been staged at dawn — and now you're paying twice for the same work.

Preparation on my sites looks like this:

  • Materials counted against the list before the crew clocks in, not discovered missing mid-morning.
  • The day's work walked on paper the night before — sequence, manpower, who owns what.
  • Weather checked, and a plan B ready when it turns.
  • Tools, consumables, and paperwork staged before they're needed.
  • The crew briefed at the gate, so nobody is guessing by 9 AM.

Poor performance is rarely a people problem. It's a preparation problem wearing a hard hat.
