Row by Row: Inspections and Documentation

Nov 2026 · LinkedIn

On a rooftop array, quality isn't inspected in at the end. It's built in row by row — and inspected the same way.

Every row gets walked before the next one covers it up. Torque marks checked. Wire management tied and supported, not draped. Bonding verified. Modules seated and clamped the way the manufacturer spec'd, not the way that was fastest.

And every row gets documented while we're standing in it. Photos. Notes tied to the drawings. Defects logged the day they're found, not the week they're remembered.

None of it works if the home runs aren't in place and managed as we go. Every row ties back to them — and if the pathways, the wire, or the plan behind them aren't ready, row-by-row discipline just documents a bottleneck. That's why lead time and proper planning matter: the inspection rhythm only holds when the work ahead of it was planned weeks out, not wished for the morning of.

Here's why it matters: a defect caught row by row costs minutes. The same defect caught at commissioning costs days — rework, re-testing, and a punch list that eats your closeout.

The paperwork isn't bureaucracy. It's the receipt that the work was done right, and it's what protects the crew that did it.

Inspect early. Document everything. Closeout takes care of itself.
